C1 Grammar · Participle Clauses

  • Present participle: An -ing clause can express reason, simultaneous action or result. Seeing the brand as part of their identity, some consumers defend it.
  • Past participle: A past participle often has a passive meaning. Exposed to a logo, volunteers then completed a task.
  • Perfect participle: Having + past participle makes the earlier action explicit. Use having been + past participle for an earlier passive action. Having compared the results, the researchers discussed their findings.
  • Check the subject: In an introductory participle clause, the implied subject normally matches the subject of the main clause. In Having seen the logo, participants recognised it later, the participants both saw and recognised it.
